  • Rolling 1-month puts deepened the worst drawdown. Premium bleed in the 2000–02 bear and expensive re-strikes at VIX 60 outweighed the payoffs.
  • Puts pay in gap crashes like COVID, much less in slow grinds like 2008, and not at all in a rates-led sell-off like 2022.
  • A volatility-target overlay saved the most drawdown per unit of cost. The practical design is a vol-target core with convex puts funded by calls or gated by trend.

Skew is the key assumption and is stressed ±30% in the repo. Model prices, not dealer quotes.

Module 4

Dashboard with an embedded LLM analyst

A Streamlit dashboard shows every module, with a live scenario repricer for the hedges. The analyst tab runs Claude in a tool-use loop over seven functions that read these results or reprice options. It may only quote numbers that come back from a tool, and each answer shows the tool calls behind it. Without an API key it falls back to deterministic routing over the same tools.

Example: “What if the S&P drops 20% and VIX goes to 50?” returns the unhedged loss, the put’s gain and the net loss, with the repricing inputs attached.

Limits
  • ETF proxies start 2002–07, giving about 22 years and three real crises. Short ETF positions stand in for futures.
  • Options are Black–Scholes with a parametric skew, not traded chains.
  • OECD 3-month rates proxy for FX forward points. This is backtested research, not a live record.
